03 THE IDEA

Reweaving What We Inherited

Unravel. Understand. Reweave.

The idea of Social Defragmentation.

Life in today's world can feel exhausting.

We face countless structural challenges, while constantly struggling with limited time, resources, and capacity.

And sometimes, even the endless demand for greater “efficiency” becomes exhausting.

When we want to change the way things are, or when change becomes unavoidable, it is easy to criticize those who came before us, or to blame the people and institutions around us.

But I would like to believe that many of the things we have today exist because someone, at some point, needed them.

Most were probably created with the intention of making something better.

So rather than simply rejecting what we have inherited, I want to ask:

How did we get here?

What purposes did these systems originally serve?

Could we take a closer look at the complexity we have accumulated, gently unravel it, and weave it together again?

Over time, systems and institutions have been layered upon one another.

And in many places, what remains resembles a patchwork of rules, exceptions, and adjustments.

A Small Example from the United Kingdom

Let's consider an example from the United Kingdom: the High Income Child Benefit Charge.

The UK provides Child Benefit to support families raising children.

That makes sense.

But should families with higher incomes receive the same level of support?

That is also a reasonable question.

So a system was introduced to recover some or all of the benefit through taxation when an individual parent's income exceeds a certain threshold.

There are understandable reasons behind this decision, too.

But here is where something curious happens.

Imagine two families.

Family A

One parent earns £75,000 a year.
The other parent has no income.
Their combined household income is £75,000.

Family B

Both parents earn £40,000 a year.
Their combined household income is £80,000.

Under the current system, Family A may have to repay part of its Child Benefit through the tax system.

Family B, meanwhile, can receive the full benefit.

Even though Family B has a higher total household income.

How did we arrive at this situation?

Each rule has its own purpose.

Each decision has its own history.

But when we step back and look at the whole system, something feels a little strange.

We have added new rules to solve new problems, often with the intention of helping people or improving society.

But what happens when all those rules are combined?

Sometimes, the result becomes far more complicated than anyone originally intended.

What About Our Calendar?

The same question can be asked of the calendar system we use today.

Calendars are indispensable to modern life.

Yet the system we have inherited through centuries of history sometimes requires surprisingly complicated adjustments.

Payment dates that shift around weekends and business days.

Daily calculations that change depending on the number of days in a month.

Annual schedules that must be rearranged year after year.

Even behind the scenes, computer systems continuously perform calculations to accommodate these variations.

What if we could “defragment” some of this complexity?

What if we could carefully unravel these layers of rules and adjustments, then reweave them into something more coherent, inclusive, and rational?

What if we could eliminate the need for certain processes, rather than simply making them more efficient?

The Equilune Calendar is one attempt to bring this idea of Social Defragmentation to life.

Before We Add Another Patch

Equilune is only one possible beginning.

There may be many other things in our everyday lives that deserve a second look.

Not because they were necessarily wrong.

But because the world has changed since they were created.

Before adding another patch, what if we took a moment to look at the whole fabric?
